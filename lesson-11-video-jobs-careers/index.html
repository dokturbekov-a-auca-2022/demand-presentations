Career Quest

Watch for real people at work. Notice the skills they use. Then design a future that sounds like you.

Jobs & Careers
Opening · 2 min: Invite students to name one job they find interesting. “I don’t know yet” is a perfect answer.
CHECK-IN · 5 MIN

Where could your curiosity take you?

Tap one card, then say: “I might enjoy a job where…”
Check-in · 5 min: Students may choose a fictional character or a future self. Avoid asking anyone to make a permanent career decision.
BRIDGE · 3 MIN

Last lesson: a postcard from tomorrow. Today: a job from tomorrow.

Future Continuous
This time next year, I will be learning new skills.
Career language
What skills will you be using in your ideal job?

Complete the question with a partner, then collect two different answers.

Bridge · 3 min: Recycle Lesson 10 lightly. Accept all realistic language; the goal is connection, not a grammar test.
LOOK · 5 MIN

What job could this person do?

Choose a question to reveal a speaking idea.
Warm-up · 5 min: Use the image as a prediction task. Do not confirm every guess; ask “What makes you think that?”
ROUTE · 2 MIN

Four stops. One career story.

Notice jobs
Watch closely
Ask questions
Pitch a path

By the end, you can…

name jobs, workplaces, and useful skills; understand a short career video; and ask polite questions about work.

Final mission

create a 45-second “career card” for a real or imagined person using job vocabulary and reasons.

Objectives · 2 min: Ask students to predict which route stop will be easiest and why.
WORDS · 7 MIN

Meet the job constellation.

vetarchitectchefgame designerengineerphotographerparamedicjournalist

Tap a job. Say: “A/An ___ usually works with ___.”

Choose a job to reveal a model sentence.
Vocabulary · 7 min: Pronounce together, then ask students to make a job–workplace link. Avoid saying a job is “for” one gender.
MATCH · 6 MIN

Where does the work happen?

vet
architect
chef
Choose all three places.
Practice · 6 min: Partners compare before checking. Extend: “A vet might also work in a zoo / on a farm.”
SKILLS · 5 MIN

Every job needs power tools.

Choose a skill, then name one job that needs it.
Skills · 5 min: Clarify that people can improve all four skills. Ask for evidence: “Why does a chef need teamwork?”
SAY IT · 4 MIN

Clap the strong beat.

Tap a word, repeat it, then use it in a sentence.
Pronunciation · 4 min: Tap the table only on capital syllables. Students then backchain “a game designer.”
BEFORE WATCHING · 3 MIN

Our mini video follows Lina.

CAREER QUEST · MINI VIDEO

“What could I become?”

Four workplaces. Four possible futures. Watch the visual story and collect clues.

Predict

Which job will need the most teamwork? Which will use the most creativity? Why?

Pre-viewing · 3 min: Explain that this is a visual short film / teacher-read narration, so students can watch without relying on external video links.
WATCH · FIRST VIEWING

Watch for the big idea.

Choose an answer after watching.
First viewing · 5 min: Read this narration once: “Lina visits four workplaces. She watches a vet care for a dog, an architect test a model, a designer improve a game, and a chef prepare a meal. She notices that each job needs knowledge, practice, and people skills.”
WATCH · SECOND VIEWING

Now collect the details.

Listen for / look for

  • one animal
  • one object for design
  • one action in a kitchen
  • one skill used by more than one job

Partner roles

Watcher A: vet + architect
Watcher B: designer + chef

Compare your clues after the video.

Watch again before you reveal.
Second viewing · 6 min: Read the narration again more slowly. Pause after each workplace if needed; pairs only need two details each.
UNDERSTAND · 5 MIN

Did you catch the clues?

Tap each statement: green = true, pink = false.
Comprehension · 5 min: Students decide individually then justify one response using a visual clue.
NOTICE · 5 MIN

Make a strong career sentence.

A / An job needs a skill because…
Choose a model, then make a new one.
Language focus · 5 min: Highlight the article: “an architect,” but “a vet.” Invite students to make one true or invented reason.
REPAIR · 6 MIN

Fix the career glitches.

Find the correct sentence.
Controlled practice · 6 min: Students whisper the corrected version first. Ask what changed: article, verb ending, plural noun.
PAIR TASK · 7 MIN

Ask to discover the secret job.

Student A chooses

vet · architect · chef · game designer

Keep your job secret. Give only yes/no answers.

Student B asks

“Do you work with people / animals / food / buildings?”
“Do you need creativity?”
“Do you work indoors?”

Swap roles after three questions. Then say one full sentence about the job.
Information gap · 7 min: Model one round. Students can use a card from the vocabulary slide or invent another accessible job.
SPEAK · 8 MIN

Become a curious interviewer.

Ask

What do you do?
Where do you work?
What skills do you use?
What do you enjoy?

Answer

I’m a fictional…
I work in / at…
I use… because…
I enjoy…

Follow up

That sounds interesting because…
Would you recommend it? Why?

02:00
Interview · 8 min: Pairs role-play an invented professional. Encourage follow-up questions, not perfect scripts.
CREATE · 10 MIN

Pitch a path that matters.

Your 45-second card

  1. Name the job.
  2. Name the workplace.
  3. Choose two skills.
  4. Say why the job matters.

Sentence runway

“A/An ___ works ___.”
“They need ___ and ___.”
“This job matters because ___.”
“I would / wouldn’t enjoy it because ___.”

Choose a real career, a possible future job, or a character from a film/game/book.
Production · 10 min: Students prepare individually, rehearse with a partner, then present. Listeners give one specific positive response.
FEEDBACK · 4 MIN

Give each pitch a skill sticker.

Tap a sticker you could give a classmate, then add a specific reason.
Feedback · 4 min: Keep feedback specific and kind: “Your reason was clear because…” Students may self-select a sticker too.
REVIEW · 5 MIN

Collect four quest sparks.

Which job usually works with animals?

Answer four questions to fill the meter.
Review · 5 min: Teams discuss for five seconds before one person taps. Ask a student to explain one correct answer.
HOMEWORK · 3 MIN

Notice work in the real world.

1 · Find

Choose a job in a safe, everyday place, a film, a game, or a book.

2 · Write

Write 4 sentences: job, workplace, two skills, and why it matters.

3 · Optional

Bring one question you would ask that person in an interview.

Homework · 3 min: No student needs to contact a real person or share family details. A fictional professional is valid.
EXIT · 3 MIN

A future is not one answer. It is an open door.

QUEST COMPLETE

Next: Second Conditional — dreams and hypothetical situations.

Exit · 3 min: Students choose one card and say or write their ending. Celebrate questions and curiosity rather than career certainty.
