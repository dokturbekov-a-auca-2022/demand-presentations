# Original artwork prompts

Both project images were created with OpenAI's built-in image generation tool
and saved locally for offline use. These are the final prompts used for the
complete radio-broadcast redesign.

## `assets/body-broadcast-studio.png`

```text
Use case: illustration-story
Asset type: 16:9 hero background for an interactive English speaking lesson
Primary request: “Frequency 103: The Body Broadcast,” an eccentric late-night community radio studio where teenage callers share ordinary health concerns and student hosts learn to listen, clarify, and respond
Scene/backdrop: a fantastical 1970s analogue radio station inside a converted attic, with circular broadcast windows, oversized frequency dials, coiled microphone cables, glowing vacuum tubes, waveform charts, cassette racks, paper call slips, acoustic wall tiles, and a tiny on-air booth
Subject: a diverse group of older teenagers as confident radio hosts interviewing callers and consulting a calm school health professional; expressive listening and conversation are central
Style/medium: screen-printed underground music poster blended with cut-paper comic art, rough halftone dots, misregistered ink layers, dry-brush edges, collage photography shapes; sophisticated and rebellious for ages 13–17, not corporate and not childish
Composition/framing: wide 16:9 broadcast-studio panorama; a huge luminous tuning dial and microphone create the focal point; layered diagonal energy and ample dark space at upper left for HTML title copy
Lighting/mood: electric, curious, safe, warmly human; red “on air” glow, ultraviolet shadows, acid-yellow highlights
Color palette: near-black plum, electric chartreuse, hot coral, cobalt blue, dusty peach, off-white
Materials/textures: coarse paper, screen-print ink, halftone grain, gaffer tape, scratched metal, translucent colored acetate
Constraints: no legible words, no logos, no watermark, no blood, no needles, no surgery, no scary illness, no photorealism, no glossy futuristic interface, no greenhouse, no theatre curtains, no vintage medical clinic
```

## `assets/caller-switchboard.png`

```text
Use case: illustration-story
Asset type: 16:9 interactive case-board artwork for an English speaking lesson
Primary request: six fictional teenage health call-ins shown as an eccentric analogue radio switchboard, designed for learners to choose a caller, describe symptoms, and ask follow-up questions
Scene/backdrop: a huge tilted 1970s radio console with six distinct illuminated circular caller channels, patch cables, cassette spools, waveform strips, speaker grilles, and paper notes
Subject: six expressive older-teen caller portraits in separate circular channels: scarf and warm drink for sore throat; exhausted at a study desk; athlete with a gently supported ankle and no visible injury; sneezing near flowers; rubbing forehead after screen time; nervous before a school presentation with hand near stomach
Style/medium: screen-printed underground music poster, cut-paper comic portraiture, rough halftone dots, bold contour lines, misregistered ink, torn-edge collage; mature and inventive for ages 13–17
Composition/framing: wide 16:9, six clearly separated caller portraits in two rows of three; large circular channel geometry and generous space around every face for clickable HTML markers
Lighting/mood: energetic late-night broadcast, compassionate, curious, slightly humorous, never frightening
Color palette: near-black plum, electric chartreuse, hot coral, cobalt blue, dusty peach, off-white
Materials/textures: coarse paper, screen-print ink, halftone grain, scratched metal, gaffer tape
Constraints: no legible words, no numbers, no logos, no watermark, no blood, no needles, no medicine brands, no diagnosis labels, no photorealism, no greenhouse, no theatre imagery
```
